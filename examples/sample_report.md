## Executive Summary

**Charging capacity, not vehicle demand, is now the binding constraint on EV adoption.**

- Public charging is scaling fast but unevenly — highway corridors and dense urban cores lead; rural and multi-unit housing lag.
- Reliability, not raw port count, is emerging as the decisive competitive axis; uptime SLAs increasingly gate network partnerships.
- Utility interconnection timelines and grid upgrades are the most underappreciated bottleneck to network expansion.

**Recommendation:** Prioritise reliability and grid-interconnection capability over headline port counts when evaluating network operators or build-out sites.

## Overview

EV charging has moved from a chicken-and-egg question to a scaling and execution question. Vehicle demand is no longer the limiting factor in most developed markets; the constraint has shifted to deploying reliable charging where and when drivers need it. That reframes the sector as an infrastructure-build story governed by siting, grid access, and uptime rather than a pure consumer-demand story.

**Market size & growth:** Low-hundreds of billions USD by the early 2030s at a ~25-30% CAGR (directional estimate — sources vary by scope and definition).

## Key Trends

- Shift in competitive emphasis from port count to reliability and uptime.
- Corridor DC fast charging maturing ahead of urban and multi-unit-dwelling coverage.
- Growing role of utilities and grid-interconnection capacity as a gating factor.
- Consolidation pressure on sub-scale independent operators.

## Competitive Landscape

- **OEM-affiliated networks** — Corridor coverage tied to vehicle ecosystems and a captive install base.
- **Independent charge-point operators** — Multi-brand interoperability; compete on footprint and reliability.
- **Utility / retail-backed entrants** — Leverage grid access and real estate; slower but structurally advantaged on interconnection.

## SWOT

### Strengths

- Strong secular demand tailwind
- Policy and subsidy support in major markets

### Weaknesses

- Thin early-stage utilisation economics
- Uneven reliability across networks

### Opportunities

- Underserved urban and rural segments
- Reliability-as-differentiator
- Depot and fleet charging

### Threats

- Grid interconnection delays
- Subsidy dependence and policy shifts
- Commoditisation of hardware

## Opportunities

- Back operators that treat uptime as a first-class product, not a maintenance afterthought.
- Target siting where grid interconnection is already de-risked.
- Fleet and depot charging offers more predictable utilisation than public retail.

## Risks

- Grid interconnection and permitting timelines can slip build-out by quarters.
- Early-stage utilisation may not support standalone unit economics without subsidy.
- Reliability failures erode driver trust and are hard to recover.
